Six Sacred Questions · 02

What?

Name the event, behavior, words, decision, or change before the mind wraps it in interpretation.

What? is the anchor question. It asks you to describe what happened in language a camera, recording, document, or neutral observer might recognize.

Instead of “They disrespected me,” What? asks: What was said or done? Instead of “Everything fell apart,” ask: What changed first? What changed next? What remains unchanged?

The cleaner the observation, the easier it becomes to test the story built around it.

What What? is not: emotional suppression. Feelings matter. Meaning matters. The point is sequencing: first establish the observable event, then examine what it meant to you.

Where it gets distorted: labels disguised as facts. “Manipulative,” “crazy,” “toxic,” “dangerous,” “perfect,” and “betrayal” may contain interpretation. They can be explored later, but they should not replace the actual behavior.

Use it under pressure: write one sentence containing only concrete behavior. Then write a second sentence beginning, “The meaning I am currently assigning to that is...” Keeping those separate protects both emotion and accuracy.

Guardian practice

Write the camera version.

Describe one difficult moment without adjectives about character or motive. Then list what you felt and what you inferred in separate lines.
