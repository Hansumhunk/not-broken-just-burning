Privacy & Data Use

Your reflection should not become
somebody else's dataset.

The current NBJB site is deliberately simple. Its interactive reflection tools run in your browser and are designed not to submit or store what you type on an NBJB server.

What the current tools do

The Forge, Flame Check-In, Pattern Map, Boundary Builder, One Stone Planner, and Flamewalker commitment operate with client-side JavaScript. Your entries exist in the page while it is open. The current code does not send those entries to an NBJB database, account system, analytics service, or email platform.

What “copy” does

When you press a copy button, the site asks your browser to place the generated text on your device clipboard. The site does not receive a copy of that text.

No account or email signup yet

There is currently no NBJB account system, newsletter database, contact form, or email signup backend. Any future signup or contact feature should be connected only after its provider, consent language, retention behavior, unsubscribe process, and privacy implications are documented here.

Third-party requests

The current pages load web fonts from Google Fonts. That means your browser may connect to Google's font infrastructure and transmit ordinary technical request information. External links, including crisis and support resources, have their own privacy practices.

Hosting and technical logs

When the site is publicly deployed, the hosting provider may process routine technical information such as IP address, browser details, request time, and requested page as part of operating and securing the service. NBJB should document the final hosting setup here before public launch.

Do not enter secrets you cannot afford to expose

Browser-only is a useful privacy design, not magic armor. Shared devices, browser extensions, malware, screenshots, clipboard history, device backups, or other local software can affect privacy. Avoid entering passwords, financial credentials, government identifiers, or other high-risk secrets into reflection tools.

NBJB privacy principles

Collect less. Explain more.

01

Do not collect sensitive reflection content merely because technology makes it possible.

02

When data collection becomes necessary, ask clearly and explain the purpose before collection.

03

Separate public community participation from private therapeutic or legal material.

04

Give people a realistic way to unsubscribe, leave, or stop sharing future data.

Current status

Privacy by limitation.

The current site intentionally does less with personal data. That will remain the default unless a future feature has a clear reason to collect more.
