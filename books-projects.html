Books & Projects

The shelves are being built
in public.

NBJB is growing into books, workbooks, learning tracks, and digital tools. This page shows real projects that already exist in manuscript, curriculum, or development form. It does not pretend unfinished work is available, and it does not invent release dates.

Publishing rule

Status should mean something.

Available will mean the finished product can actually be delivered. Editorial Review means a substantial manuscript exists but still needs work. In Development means the project has a real architecture or source body and is actively being built.

No fake preorder buttons. No countdown clock to a date nobody has earned yet.

Legacy manuscript under review

A real manuscript is not automatically a finished product.

The first legacy NBJB book being reviewed for modern publication is the manuscript most directly tied to the movement itself.

Editorial Review

Not Broken Just Burning: The Sacred Laws of Survival

An early long-form NBJB manuscript built around emotional boundaries, pattern recognition, Sacred Mirror Work, reclaiming voice, and building a Circle of support.

Current work: remove duplicated drafting material, bring certainty and safety language up to the current NBJB standard, preserve the strongest practices, and complete a modern editorial pass before any release.

Editorial Redevelopment

DARVO: The Hidden Architecture of Abuse and Collapse

A substantial legacy manuscript examining denial, attack, reversal, narrative conflict, institutional dynamics, and psychological collapse.

Current work: sourcing, privacy, claim calibration, clinical/legal boundary review, and conversion from case-specific certainty into grounded educational pattern analysis.

The Wounded Boy Workbook

A practical companion built around the Father Imprint Map, Maternal Imprint Map, Silence Inventory, Armor Audit, Strength Model Audit, Needs Permission Map, and Chosen Manhood Code.

In Development

Riding the Roller Coaster While Shuffling the Deck

A book/workbook concept using two linked models: the roller coaster as a shared life track and the 52-card deck as a way to explore individual variation in how people move through similar events.

Current work: recovering the original source manuscript and consolidating the surviving model into a current NBJB structure.
