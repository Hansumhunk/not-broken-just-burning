Stories From the Fire · Reciprocity

Reciprocity Before Exhaustion.

Do not wait until you are completely empty to ask whether the connection has room for your needs too.

Some imbalances are temporary. One person gets sick. One person loses a job. One person is grieving. Healthy relationships can carry uneven weight for a season.

The problem is not that the scales ever tip. The problem is when one person becomes the permanent giver and exhaustion is the first evidence they allow themselves to consider.

Reciprocity does not mean keeping a petty scoreboard. It means there is enough mutuality that care can move in more than one direction. Your needs are not an interruption. Your limits are not betrayal. Your voice matters before you reach the point of collapse.

Do not make depletion the price of discovering whether someone can meet you halfway.

If you repeatedly give until resentment appears, the better question may arrive earlier: What happens when I ask for something? What happens when I say no? What happens when I stop overfunctioning?

Those moments reveal structure. A relationship that only works when you carry more than your share may not be balanced; it may simply be dependent on your overextension.

Reciprocity also applies beyond romance. Friendships, family relationships, work partnerships, and communities all become healthier when contribution and consideration flow in more than one direction.

Measure mutuality before burnout. Ask whether the relationship makes room for two people, not whether you can survive carrying it alone.

Reflection

What happens when you stop doing the extra work?

Notice who checks in, who adjusts, who asks what you need, and who only notices you when your usefulness changes.
