The Six Sacred
Questions.

Use the six questions as a working reflection. Type what you currently understand about the people, event, timing, context, explanation, and process. The goal is not to prove a case. It is to slow confusion down enough to see what belongs where.

How to use it

Build the map before demanding the answer.

Who + What + When + Where help establish the situation and context. Why explores possible meaning or motive without pretending uncertainty is proof. How traces the process and looks for the next part you can actually influence.

You do not need perfect answers. Write what you understand now, mark uncertainty honestly, and revise when better information appears.

A useful order

Do not let Why swallow the other five.

When a situation hurts, the mind often jumps straight to Why did they do this? Sometimes that question matters. But Who, What, When, and Where usually give Why better ground to stand on. How then turns the reflection toward process and the next controllable step.

A question is not a verdict

Use the six to organize your viewpoint, not manufacture certainty.

The Six Sacred Questions can help a client organize a personal reflection. They do not verify allegations, prove motive, diagnose another person, establish legal facts, or replace professional investigation, medical care, mental-health care, legal advice, or emergency support.
