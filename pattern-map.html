Pattern Map

Notice repetition.
Do not manufacture certainty.

Patterns can be useful signals, but repetition alone does not prove motive, intent, or cause. This tool helps you organize what happened, what repeated, what changed, what evidence exists, and what you may still be assuming.

Private by design: your entries stay in this browser session.

What this tool helps separate

A repeated pattern can guide investigation without becoming proof by itself.

The Pattern Map is for situations where several events seem connected and you need a cleaner way to examine the sequence. It organizes what happened before and after an anchor event, what has repeated, what changed, what evidence can actually be verified, and what remains interpretation.

This matters when stress makes repetition feel like certainty. A pattern may be important, but the useful question is not only whether something repeated. It is also whether the circumstances were comparable, whether exceptions exist, what source supports each detail, and what conclusion the available evidence can reasonably carry.
