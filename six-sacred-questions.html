The Six Sacred
Questions.

When emotion becomes fog, return to questions that can be named, placed, tested, and understood. Who? What? When? Where? Why? How? They are not mystical answers. They are a discipline for slowing the story down.

Start with Who?Take It to The Forge

The discipline

Do not ask Why first.

Why is usually the question the nervous system wants answered fastest, but motive is often the least observable part of a difficult situation. Start by identifying the people and sources, naming what happened, placing it in time and context, and only then test possible explanations.

Who + What + When + Where build the map. Why tests explanation. How traces the mechanism and points toward the next controllable action.

01

Who?

Identify people, roles, sources, witnesses, decision-makers, and who actually knows what firsthand.

Why?

Examine possible explanations and motives as hypotheses that require evidence, not feelings promoted into verdicts.

How?

Trace the process, mechanism, escalation, repair, and the first next step that actually remains within your control.

Quick pass

Run one situation through all six.

Who: Who is directly involved, and who is only interpreting?

What: What happened that I can describe without guessing motive?

When: What is the sequence, frequency, and relevant exception?

Where: What context, setting, audience, or channel matters?

Why: What explanation am I considering, and what evidence would support or weaken it?

How: How did the process unfold, and what can I influence next?

A question is not a verdict

The goal is disciplined curiosity.

The Six Sacred Questions can organize a personal reflection. They do not prove motive, diagnose people, establish legal facts, or replace professional investigation, medical care, mental-health care, or emergency support.
