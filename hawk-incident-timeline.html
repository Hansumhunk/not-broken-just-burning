Working application · offline first

Keep your records in your hands.

Download the self-contained HTML file, then open it from your device with your browser. No external scripts, accounts, analytics or network requests are needed inside the tool. Entries stay in memory until you explicitly export a file. Close or reload the tool without exporting and unsaved entries are lost. Exports are readable, unencrypted files; use secure storage and avoid shared devices.

1. Build a chronology before you build a conclusion

When a marriage ends under pressure, ten things can seem to happen at once. A change in a parenting schedule, an unexpected bill, a painful message and a court notice may all land in the same afternoon. You remember the feeling of that afternoon more clearly than the order of the events. A week later you may remember one conversation as happening before another, even though the messages show the opposite. This is a practical problem, not a character defect. A timeline gives those events individual places so you can examine them without having to carry the whole situation in your head.

Start with a narrow question. Perhaps you need to understand when a schedule changed or prepare for a meeting about household expenses. You do not need to document every difficult exchange from the first day of your relationship. A question puts a boundary around the work. It also helps you decide which events are relevant and which belong in a private journal rather than the chronology.

The purpose is not to manufacture a narrative that wins. It is to create an account that remains useful even when a detail turns out to be inconvenient for you. If the timeline changes your understanding, that is evidence that the process is doing its job. Think of the chronology as a workbench: events go on it in order, while theories wait elsewhere.

Hard Evidence · 2 / 17

2. Choose the scope and establish a starting point

Write a one sentence purpose before opening a record. For example: I want to establish what happened with the agreed pickup schedule between March 1 and March 20. That is more manageable than I want to establish everything that went wrong during the marriage. The first question has a defined time window, a specific subject and a reasonable stopping point. The second can expand forever.

Choose the date range based on the question, not on how upset you feel. Include earlier context if it actually explains an event, and label it as background. If the issue is a recent missed appointment, an argument three years earlier may be personally significant without contributing anything to this task. Keep it out unless there is a specific reason to connect it. A larger archive may have several separate chronologies instead of one immense document.

Set a practical limit on each work session. Twenty minutes spent entering three accurate events is often more productive than three hours spent searching old messages while growing increasingly angry. Put an end time on your calendar. Decide what you will do afterward, such as preparing dinner, taking a walk or getting ready for tomorrow. Documentation is something you do; it should not become the role you occupy throughout the day.

Hard Evidence · 3 / 17

3. Learn the difference between the event and the record

A timeline entry is a description of an event. A supporting record is something that may help establish what occurred. These are not interchangeable. An entry saying you received a message at seven in the evening is not the message itself. A screenshot labeled E001 may support the entry, but a description of the screenshot does not reproduce the entire exchange. An original message export might include details that a cropped image leaves out.

Keep separate places for the event date and the date you created your note. If you made a note on May 10 about a conversation on May 6, both dates matter. Do not make the note appear to have been written on May 6. If the date is uncertain, record that uncertainty. You are allowed to write early May, exact day unknown. A precise looking date that you guessed is worse than an honestly approximate one.

The builder gives each event a stable reference. You can associate an event with a document reference without attaching the document. One event might have several related records, and one record might help explain multiple events. A later evidence index can organize those relationships. For now, the important distinction is that your account describes what you know, while the underlying material remains in its original secure location.

Hard Evidence · 4 / 17

4. Capture facts in language that another person can understand

Try writing each observation as if the person reading it has never met either adult. Avoid nicknames, shorthand judgments and references to previous arguments that only you recognize. A reader needs to know the action, relevant context and how you learned about it. You do not need to persuade them that your description is correct through adjectives.

Compare these two sentences. The first says: She once again sabotaged my parenting time because she wants control. The second says: The agreed pickup was scheduled for Friday at five; at four thirty I received a message requesting a change to Saturday morning. I replied asking whether Friday was still possible. The second description provides something a reader can examine. The first combines a possible pattern, a motive and a judgment into a single claim without describing what occurred.

This does not require you to deny your emotional response. If you felt anxious or angry, that belongs in an impact field or your private reflection. Feelings are real experiences, but they do not determine what another person intended. Keep the factual description plain, then separately record the practical effect: you had arranged transportation and needed to change your plans. That is more informative than a general statement that the other person ruined your whole weekend.

Hard Evidence · 5 / 17

5. Classify the source and preserve honest uncertainty

Ask how you know each detail. Did you directly see it? Did you read a record? Did another person tell you? Are you recalling it from memory? Or is it your interpretation of several events? Each source has different limitations. A forwarded message may lack context; a recollection may omit wording; a third party may have misunderstood what happened. Classifying a source is not the same as declaring it reliable or unreliable.

Write down what you directly observed and what was reported to you as separate statements. If a neighbor says that a vehicle was in a driveway, your observation is that the neighbor told you this; you did not personally see the vehicle. Do not turn their report into your firsthand account. If you later receive a photograph, you can reference it as an additional record and still distinguish what the photograph shows from what you infer about it.

When there are disagreements, keep the disagreement visible. An entry can say the message indicates one time and my calendar says another; I have not resolved the discrepancy. You do not have to choose a winner today. Unresolved questions are useful because they identify where you might need to consult a record or ask a qualified professional. A chronology becomes stronger through transparent limitations, not by covering them up.

6. Work with dates, times and ambiguity

Record the most precise date you can support. If you know the day but not the time, record the day only. If you know the month but not the day, use month precision and explain the source of the estimate. If you cannot establish a date at all, keep the event in the unknown date group rather than forcing it into a sequence. The order of uncertain events can remain uncertain.

Be mindful of time zones, daylight saving changes and message applications that display time differently on different devices. If a document already includes a local time or time zone, preserve that detail as written. Do not silently convert times and then present the converted value as if it appeared on the original record. In this tool, a time is a local clock value; any time zone or time uncertainty belongs in the context field. A professional can help reconcile more complex timing questions if they matter.

Sorting assists you in seeing the order but does not establish the exact sequence when multiple events share the same date without times. Do not assume that the order in which rows appear means one event necessarily preceded another. Record an explicit sequence only when you have a basis for it. The system deliberately distinguishes approximate and unknown dates so it does not give a false appearance of certainty.

Hard Evidence · 7 / 17

7. Preserve originals instead of improving them

Work only with information you have permission to access. You can organize your own communications, documents and records that have been supplied to you lawfully. A difficult relationship does not create permission to access another adult's private accounts, devices, location or correspondence. Do not guess passwords, secretly track someone, impersonate anyone or use the timeline as a reason to start monitoring another person.

Keep the original materials intact. If you make a working excerpt or redact a copy for discussion, mark it as a copy and keep the original separate. Do not improve a screenshot by removing material that changes the meaning. A cropped image can be useful for orientation, but the complete conversation may be needed to interpret it fairly. Do not delete material because it makes your position uncomfortable.

Use reference codes rather than uploading files to this tool. A code like E001 can refer to a complete message export in your own secure storage. Keep a separate note of where the original is stored. If your attorney has given specific preservation instructions, follow those instructions rather than inventing a new process. Do not use editing or deletion features in a planning tool to destroy underlying records or information subject to preservation duties. Questions about admissibility, discovery, recording laws and record retention belong with qualified counsel.

Hard Evidence · 8 / 17

8. Assign categories without turning them into accusations

Categories help you find events later. Parenting, communication, finances, legal administration, housing and other are neutral categories that describe the subject. They should not be labels of guilt. An event can be classified as communication without declaring that the communication was harassment. A financial event can be recorded without deciding whether it constitutes fraud, deception or a criminal offense.

You may notice that an event affects multiple parts of life. A pickup change might affect work and transportation as well as parenting. Choose the primary category for the purpose of the timeline and explain the secondary impact in the entry. Do not create multiple identical records merely so the same event shows up under every category. A clean chronology can be reviewed by someone who does not know your personal classification system.

If you believe an event might have legal significance, write down the question you want to ask your attorney rather than assigning your own legal finding. For example, ask whether a particular payment should be included in a disclosure, rather than declaring the transaction unlawful. You can be firm about the importance of an observation while remaining accurate about what it does and does not establish. Neutral categories also make it easier to include ordinary events, resolutions and corrections, which may matter as much as conflict.

Hard Evidence · 9 / 17

9. Document parenting without turning children into sources of conflict

A father under pressure may feel driven to prove every good thing he does. Parenting notes can be useful for practical continuity: school events, agreed exchanges, appointments, transportation, meals, routines and other responsibilities. Record relevant facts, not a contest between parents. A note that a child attended an appointment with you is more useful than a note that claims you are the only parent who cares.

If a child says something relevant to safety or their needs, focus first on their wellbeing. Document only what is necessary, including the context and what you did next. Do not repeatedly question a child to get a preferred answer, tell them what to say, recruit them as an investigator or ask them to carry messages between adults. A child's statement can be meaningful without becoming a conclusion about the other household. Seek appropriate professional guidance for concerning disclosures and act promptly when immediate safety is at stake.

Use aliases or minimal descriptions wherever possible. Full names, school addresses, medical information and private images should not be entered into a general-purpose chronology if they are unnecessary. The goal of the work is a stable life for the child, not a record of points scored against another parent. Ask whether the next action helps the child today. If it does not, reconsider whether the event needs your attention right now.

10. Scenario one: a disputed exchange

Imagine Jordan and Alex have an agreed Friday pickup at five. At four twenty, Jordan receives a message asking to move the exchange to six thirty. Jordan feels that this is another example of being treated as unimportant and wants to send an angry response. Instead, Jordan records the scheduled time, the actual message time, the requested change and the response sent. Jordan waits until the exchange takes place before recording the actual outcome.

A useful entry might read: Friday, April 10, four twenty p.m., message received requesting pickup at six thirty instead of five. At four thirty Jordan replied asking for confirmation. The exchange occurred at six thirty-five. Supporting references: E001, full message conversation; E002, calendar entry. Impact: transportation arrangements were changed and Jordan arrived home later than planned. Unknown: reason for the request unless supported by a message or other direct information.

Notice that the record includes the resolution. If the message explained a work emergency, that context should not disappear simply because Jordan was inconvenienced. If a similar event occurs later, it can be entered independently. The timeline is not a place to settle which adult is a good person. It helps establish what happened, what affected the schedule and what needs to be discussed with counsel or addressed through a permitted communication channel.

11. Scenario two: an unexplained financial transaction

Suppose Morgan reviews a bank statement that Morgan is authorized to access. A payment appears that Morgan does not recognize. It is tempting to enter a claim that the other party has taken money. Instead, record the date on the statement, amount if relevant, the description displayed by the bank and the fact that the purpose is not yet known. Use a reference pointing to the original statement in secure storage, leaving account numbers and unnecessary personal financial details outside this tool.

Later, a receipt may clarify that the payment was for a shared household expense. That new record should be added as a development rather than quietly rewriting the initial question as if there had never been uncertainty. If the receipt does not match the transaction, preserve both materials and prepare a clear question about the discrepancy. There is no need to decide that a criminal act occurred simply because the records do not yet reconcile.

This approach helps distinguish a financial observation from an accounting interpretation. A lawyer or qualified accounting professional can advise on classification, obligations and what further records are appropriate to request. The timeline does not replace those functions. It makes the conversation more efficient by giving each question a date, a source and a clearly described gap. You may discover a mundane explanation, a genuine unresolved matter or something that requires further advice.

12. Scenario three: a remembered conversation

Imagine Taylor remembers a difficult conversation in a kitchen several months ago. The exact date is unclear. Taylor remembers the subject and the emotional effect but cannot reproduce the exact wording. Instead of putting quotation marks around reconstructed language, Taylor enters the approximate month, identifies the source as personal recollection and writes a summary explicitly marked as a recollection. The event remains approximate until other reliable information helps establish its date.

Taylor later finds a calendar entry showing a meeting during the relevant week. The calendar does not record the conversation itself. It may help narrow when the conversation might have happened, but it does not establish the words spoken. Taylor can add the calendar as a related reference while noting its limitations. If the chronology shows that the remembered date conflicts with the calendar, Taylor should not force the records into agreement.

The same approach works when a friend recalls a conversation differently. Record your own recollection and keep the friend's account distinct. Do not pressure the friend to adopt your version or supply missing details. A useful timeline has room for imperfect knowledge. An honest statement that you cannot remember an exact sentence is more informative than a confidently invented quotation. Your past experience still matters even when some of its details cannot be reconstructed precisely.

13. Corrections are part of disciplined documentation

You will occasionally discover that an earlier entry is incomplete or incorrect. Perhaps a date was transposed, a record was assigned the wrong reference, or you originally attributed a statement to the wrong person. The appropriate response is to correct the working timeline and explain the reason for the change, not defend the old entry because you already wrote it.

Keep previous exported versions when appropriate and make a short correction note. For example: On May 18 I corrected the date from May 12 to May 13 after reviewing E004. The tool retains a stable event reference when you edit it and records the time of the last edit. It is not an immutable audit log; you need separate secure versioned copies if a reliable historical record is important. Do not mistake an editable planning tool for an official evidence repository.

If an entry was made in anger and includes unnecessary judgment, you can revise your working description into neutral language. Preserve underlying originals and ask counsel before changing or removing material that may need to be retained. Corrections help keep a working document accurate, but they should not conceal changes from anyone who has a legitimate reason to understand them. A willingness to correct yourself is consistent with the Guardian values of honesty and responsibility.

14. Review the chronology without hunting for patterns

Once you have a small set of events, read them from beginning to end. Identify which fields are supported, which are recollections and which are questions. Look for missing context before looking for similarities. Did a later message resolve the earlier concern? Were there occasions when the same situation went smoothly? Are there relevant events you left out because they did not fit your initial interpretation?

Patterns can help you choose a question worth investigating, but repeated dates or similar wording do not automatically establish a common motive or coordinated plan. A timeline provides a sequence, not proof of causation. If two things happened close together, you can record that relationship without deciding one caused the other. The Forge and Pattern Map already provide structured ways to examine your interpretation separately from the record.

Set a deliberate review rhythm instead of rereading the chronology whenever you feel unsettled. For example, review before a scheduled professional appointment or when a relevant new event occurs. You do not need to reopen it every evening. When reviewing no longer produces a practical next action, close the tool. A chronology should free up attention for the rest of your life. If it continually increases distress or keeps you from work, sleep or parenting, change how you are using it and consider reaching out to qualified support.

15. Prepare a concise handoff and protect sensitive material

Before a meeting with your attorney, choose the issue you need help with. A full archive may not be the best starting document. A short set of relevant entries, with references to originals, can help you ask a specific question. Let counsel determine what materials are necessary, how to share them securely and whether to include anything in a formal filing. The timeline is an organizational aid, not a prepared affidavit, legal submission or determination of admissibility.

The offline builder does not send entries to NBJB. It keeps them in memory during the session, and you can explicitly export a JSON file for later reopening. That file is ordinary readable text, not encrypted. A person with access to the file may be able to read it. Save it only on a device and in a location you control, ideally using appropriate encrypted storage and access controls. Check whether your downloads folder is synchronized to a shared cloud account. Avoid shared or monitored devices if doing so is unsafe.

Printing produces another copy of the information. Be mindful of printers, shared output trays and anyone who can access the resulting pages. If you are unsure about the safest way to preserve records in an abusive situation, seek qualified safety guidance. Privacy is not achieved simply by calling a tool private; it depends on the environment in which you use it.

16. Guided exercise: build your first three entries

Use invented details or a low sensitivity situation to practice before handling real records. Choose one small issue and write its purpose outside the tool. Identify three events: an initial agreement, a subsequent change and a final resolution. Enter the events one at a time, using the date precision you can actually support. Give each a short neutral description and select its source type. Add a reference such as E001 only if you have a corresponding record.

Next, identify one practical impact and one remaining question. Do not force yourself to add an impact when there was none, and do not force a question when the issue is resolved. Review the results in chronological order. Edit one entry to simulate discovering a mistake. Add a correction explanation, check that the event reference stays the same and confirm that the corrected entry still makes sense alongside the others.

Export your practice data and reopen it in a fresh copy of the tool. Confirm that all three event references, dates and notes survive. Try the category filter and check that it does not silently remove entries from the exported file. Close the tool when finished and choose one real life task that has nothing to do with reviewing your past. The objective is to know how to use the system and how to stop using it. That is the beginning of disciplined action, not another obligation to be perfect.

Hard Evidence · 17 / 17

17. From organization to agency

The Hard Evidence phase is not complete because you have a large quantity of material. It is working when you can understand the sequence, identify the sources, acknowledge the unknowns and ask a better question. Three well organized entries may be more useful than hundreds of notes without dates or context. Quantity is not a substitute for judgment.

When an outside event requires a practical response, connect the chronology to a next action. That might be confirming a meeting, organizing a record you already possess, asking an attorney a specific question or making a plan for a child's routine. If nothing needs action, the appropriate outcome might be simply recording the event and returning to your day. Do not create confrontation merely to obtain more information or force an answer. You are allowed to leave some questions unresolved.

The larger H.A.W.K. framework will connect this work with the Evidence Index, support network, communication preparation and other tools. Those tools share the same purpose: make necessary work clearer, not keep a person living in conflict. Meanwhile, the Flamewalker Path addresses the internal work that a chronology cannot perform. It can help you examine feelings, rebuild boundaries and decide what you want your life to become. Your identity is not a stack of records. Keep the record useful, keep your actions deliberate and keep building a future beyond the case.
