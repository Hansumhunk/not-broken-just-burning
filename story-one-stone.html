Stories From the Fire · Overwhelm

One Stone at a Time.

The mountain becomes impossible when the mind tries to lift the whole thing at once.

Overwhelm has a strange talent for merging separate problems into one giant object. The bill, the phone call, the paperwork, the appointment, the conversation, the broken thing in the driveway, the unanswered email, and tomorrow’s responsibility become one enormous sentence: “Everything is too much.”

Once that happens, the brain is no longer choosing between seven tasks. It is standing in front of a mountain.

The solution is not always motivation. Sometimes the solution is decomposition.

What actually has to happen first? What can wait until tomorrow? What can be delegated? What is uncomfortable but not urgent? What is not even a task, but a fear about a task?

You do not move a mountain. You move the next stone.

One Stone does not pretend the mountain is small. It refuses to make size an excuse for paralysis. If the next action is a phone call, make the phone call. If the next action is opening the document, open the document. If the next action is eating before making any decisions because your body is running on fumes, then food is the stone.

This is not laziness disguised as wisdom. It is sequencing.

A nervous system under pressure can treat every unfinished item as equally urgent. Deliberately deciding what will not be handled right now is part of the method. “Not today” is different from “never.”

Progress becomes easier to see when the unit of measurement gets smaller. You may still have a mountain tomorrow. But there can also be one less stone in it.

Reflection

What is the mountain, and what is one stone you can move next?

Name what can intentionally wait. Giving something a later place can stop it from screaming for attention now.
