Founder Story · Rickey Partin Jr.

I did not build this
from untouched ground.

Not Broken Just Burning came out of a period when survival, identity, fatherhood, trust, shame, fear, and the future all seemed to be sitting on the same fault line. I did not start with a polished philosophy. I started by trying to understand what was happening inside me and how to keep moving without letting pain decide who I would become.

Why some details are missing

The people in this story do not need to be named for the lessons to be real.

Some family and legal matters in my life are still active. This page intentionally leaves out names, accusations, case details, private records, and identifying information about other people. I can tell the truth about my own fear, shame, choices, mistakes, survival, and rebuilding without turning an unfinished private conflict into public spectacle.

The Fire

There was a point when life stopped feeling like life and started feeling like survival.

I was carrying pressure from several directions at once. Relationships, family, money, identity, trust, and institutions I expected to make things clearer instead became part of the chaos. I became exhausted in a way sleep could not fix.

At my lowest, I was not looking for a brand, a movement, or some heroic origin story. I wanted peace. I wanted to be present as a father. I wanted the fear to stop owning every decision about tomorrow.

That period forced me to confront an uncomfortable truth: surviving something does not automatically teach you what it meant. Survival can keep you alive while leaving the story scrambled.

The Turn

I started treating my own life like evidence that needed to be understood.

I wrote. I journaled. I documented timelines. I questioned my own assumptions. I looked for patterns, then forced myself to ask whether the pattern was actually supported by evidence. I sought help. I studied trauma, boundaries, shame, relationships, behavior, symbolism, and the strange ways a human mind tries to survive prolonged stress.

The questions became as important as the answers: Who? What? When? Where? Why? How? What do I actually know? What am I assuming? What belongs to me to repair? What am I carrying that never should have become my identity?

That process did not make every painful feeling disappear. It gave those feelings somewhere to go besides the driver's seat.

Shame

One of the hardest fires was learning not to use my own humanity as evidence against myself.

I had to examine the questions that sound responsible but can quietly become self-punishment: How did I let this happen? Why did I stay? Why did I react that way? Why did I keep hoping?

I learned that accountability and shame are not the same thing. I can own a choice without turning it into a sentence about my worth. Loyalty is not the same thing as stupidity. Love is not the same thing as blindness. A survival response is not a complete character reference.

That distinction became one of the foundations of Sacred Mirror Work and of the way NBJB approaches rebuilding: tell the truth, own what is yours, and stop volunteering to carry what is not.

Rebuilding

The tools on this site came from problems I actually had to work through.

Fire Reclamation came from needing to tell the story without living inside it forever. The Guardian's Path came from learning the difference between protection and control. Sacred Mirror Work came from confronting shame and distorted inner narratives. Masculine Restoration came from asking what strength should look like when it is not domination or silence.

Voice of Fire came from learning that speaking is not the same thing as fighting. Circle of the Flame came from recognizing that isolation can feel safe while quietly becoming another cage.

The Forge, Pattern Map, Boundary Builder, Check-In, and One Stone tools grew from the same practical need: when your mind is overloaded, you need a way to slow the story down enough to see what is actually in front of you.

Why NBJB exists

I wanted the things I learned in survival to become useful somewhere other than my own head.

The earliest mission behind Not Broken Just Burning was to awaken, guide, and protect through lived experience, emotional healing, spiritual awareness, and psychological truth. Over time I learned to make that promise more grounded.

NBJB does not protect people by becoming their authority. It tries to help people strengthen their own agency, boundaries, discernment, language, voice, and access to appropriate support. It does not ask anyone to believe my story in order to use a useful question.

If something I learned in the worst season of my life can help someone else name what they feel, challenge shame, build one healthy boundary, ask for help, or choose one honest next step, then the fire produced something besides ashes.

What I can honestly claim

I am a founder with lived experience. I am not finished.

I am not presenting myself as a therapist, physician, attorney, prophet, or person with a perfect answer for somebody else's life. I am a father and a person who went through a severe period of emotional collapse and rebuilding, then chose to study the process instead of hiding from it.

I still have hard days. I still revise what I think when better evidence appears. I still have to practice the values on this site rather than merely print them in orange letters.

That is the credibility I want NBJB to have: not perfection, but proximity to the work.

The story is still being written

I walked through the fire. I am still learning what to carry out of it.

The movement exists because survival was not enough for me. I wanted understanding. Then I wanted rebuilding. Eventually I wanted whatever I learned to become useful to somebody else.
